Welcome & Course Guide

GenAI-Powered Data Analysis for Urban Mobility

Mobillity EIT Urban Mobility Co-funded by the European Union

Welcome! In GenAI-Powered Data Analysis for Urban Mobility, you'll learn how to use large language models, Python, and real European mobility datasets to explore, analyse, and communicate urban transport insights. From understanding how LLMs work — and where they fail — to producing data-driven stories that stakeholders can act on, you'll gain both the AI fluency and the hands-on analytical skills needed to turn messy mobility data into evidence for smarter, safer, and more sustainable cities.

What You'll Achieve

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  • Explain how LLM constraints — context windows, knowledge cutoffs, and hallucinations — shape the reliability of any data analysis you run through them.
  • Use LLMs to load, inspect, and clean real urban mobility datasets in Google Colab.
  • Generate and iteratively refine Python analysis and visualisation code through LLM-assisted workflows, using pandas, matplotlib, and friends.
  • Communicate analytical findings by combining results with AI-assisted summarisation and visualisation for stakeholder audiences.
  • Critically evaluate LLM-generated outputs by cross-checking against source data — catching fabricated patterns, statistical errors, and misleading charts.

How the Course Works

  • Six modules of focused, hands-on technical content.
  • Video lectures in every module explain the concepts and demonstrate the workflows step by step.
  • Hands-on notebooks in Google Colab walk you through real applications, so you can follow along and run the analysis yourself.
  • Exercises let you explore on your own and practise the skills on real mobility data.
  • Quizzes at the end of each module help you check and reinforce what you've learned.
  • Certification is earned through the final exam alone — score at least 50% to pass.

How you earn your certificate

The module quizzes are there to help you learn — they don't count towards your certificate. To be certified, you only need to pass the final exam with a score of at least 50%.
