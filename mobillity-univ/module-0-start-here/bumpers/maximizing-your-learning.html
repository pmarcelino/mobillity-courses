Maximizing Your Learning

Five evidence-based strategies to get more from this course

Mobillity EIT Urban Mobility Co-funded by the European Union

Self-paced learning gives you the freedom to choose when, where, and how you learn — but freedom also comes with responsibility.

Why it matters

Research in cognitive science, educational psychology, and attention management shows that small, deliberate changes in how you engage with a MOOC can significantly improve comprehension, retention, and focus.

Below are five practical, evidence-based strategies you can apply immediately while taking this course.

1. Use 1.5–2× Playback Selectively (Slow Down Only for Dense Bits)

Why: Studies show that moderate speed increases (1.5×–2×) do not harm comprehension for most content, and can save time. Slowing down for complex material maximizes understanding.

How to implement
  1. Set your default playback speed to 1.5× or 2×.
  2. When you encounter new or difficult concepts, temporarily reduce to 1.0×.
  3. Before assessments, consider a fast (2×) second viewing of key material for reinforcement.
  4. Avoid speeds above 2× — research indicates they may reduce comprehension.

Reference Murphy, J., Sala, G., & Castel, A. D. (2022). The effects of lecture video speed on immediate and delayed comprehension. Applied Cognitive Psychology, 36(1), 77–85. https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3850

2. Control the Soundscape (Quiet or Isolate Speech with Headphones/Earplugs)

Why: Background noise — especially speech — competes for your brain’s language-processing resources, lowering comprehension and recall.

How to implement
  1. Choose the quietest space available.
  2. Use closed-back or noise-cancelling headphones if background noise is unavoidable.
  3. Play neutral background noise (white/brown noise) only if it helps you focus.

Reference Banbury, S. P., & Berry, D. C. (1998). Disruption of office-related tasks by speech and office noise. British Journal of Psychology, 89(3), 499–517. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2044-8295.1998.tb02699.x

3. Put the Phone in Another Room

Why: Even a silent phone on your desk can reduce available working memory and problem-solving capacity.

How to implement
  1. Before study sessions, physically move your phone to another room or closed drawer.
  2. Turn off notifications on your computer during learning.
  3. Keep it out of sight until your break.

Reference Ward, A. F., Duke, K., Gneezy, A., & Bos, M. W. (2017). Brain drain: The mere presence of one’s own smartphone reduces available cognitive capacity. Journal of the Association for Consumer Research, 2(2), 140–154. https://doi.org/10.1086/691462

4. Pomodoro-Style Focus with Real Breaks

Why: Working in focused intervals with short breaks helps maintain energy, prevent mental fatigue, and sustain attention.

How to implement
  1. Set a timer for 25–50 minutes of focused work.
  2. Take a 5–10 minute break away from your screen.
  3. After 3–4 cycles, take a longer 20–30 minute break.
  4. Use breaks for movement, hydration, or relaxation — not social media.

Reference Ariga, A., & Lleras, A. (2011). Brief and rare mental “breaks” keep you focused: Deactivation and reactivation of task goals preempt vigilance decrements. Cognition, 118(3), 439–443. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cognition.2010.12.007

5. Prefer Generative Notes (Longhand or Disciplined Typing)

Why: Taking notes in your own words forces deeper processing, which improves understanding and recall.

How to implement
  1. Whether handwriting or typing, avoid verbatim transcription.
  2. Summarize ideas in your own words; add headings, diagrams, and questions.
  3. At the end of each video, condense notes into a one-page summary.

Reference Mueller, P. A., & Oppenheimer, D. M. (2014). The pen is mightier than the keyboard: Advantages of longhand over laptop note taking. Psychological Science, 25(6), 1159–1168. https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614524581

The bottom line

Applying even two or three of these strategies consistently can help you retain more, understand better, and finish this MOOC with stronger results.
