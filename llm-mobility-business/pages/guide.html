Welcome & Course Guide

Working an Engagement with a Free-Tier Chat Assistant: A Course for Mobility Consultancies, Operators and Technology Providers

Urban mobility Research, data and tenders Browser chat assistants

Welcome! In Working an Engagement with a Free-Tier Chat Assistant, you'll learn how to do the research, data and tender work of a live mobility engagement with the browser chat assistant you already have, and to do it in a way you can defend to a client. One engagement runs through the course: a consultancy preparing a bid for a city's on-demand and shared-mobility service. The artefacts travel with you — the tender pack and its published evaluation rubric, the client's fleet operations log (held under NDA), the city's KPI and service exports, and public competitor material. Every lesson starts from a named task on one of those artefacts and works it step by step: what we are doing, why, and what the assistant is doing with what we give it. By the end you'll know which parts of a task the assistant can carry, which parts stay yours to verify, and how to make sure nothing reaches a client that you cannot trace to a source, a page, or a computation log.

What You'll Achieve

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  • Predict where an answer will come from — training memory, web search, or a file you attached — before you rely on it.
  • Classify each item in a client or partner file as pasteable, redact-first, or never-paste, under the tool's data-use terms and the engagement's confidentiality terms.
  • Produce an evidence file in which every claim carries its source, a quote from that source, and a date.
  • Detect claims the cited source does not support in a research brief, before the brief is used.
  • Confirm a cleaning change list — every row and value a tool dropped, merged, or reformatted in a messy spreadsheet export — against the source file.
  • Produce a figure and a chart you can defend from exports of different sources: the computation log attached, every plotted figure traced to a cell, and the check stopped at the first step whose total or row count no longer matches the source.
  • Extract a requirement matrix from a tender pack, schema-first, with a page citation on every row.
  • Draft a proposal section whose every claim traces to the matrix, the evidence file, or a checked figure, and score that draft against the tender's published rubric.
  • Turn the drafted section into a client slide outline — one message per slide, every figure carried unchanged and traceable to the computation log or the evidence file.
  • Hold the engagement in one shared project per client — a brief, house rules, the evidence file and a decision log, each with a master copy you own — so nothing reaches another client's project, and design a repeatable multi-document workflow with a coverage check.
  • Write the one client disclosure line from the decision log of what the tool produced and what was checked.

Who This Course Is For

People who do research, data and tender work in mobility businesses: consultants, transport planners, strategy analysts, project managers and business developers at consultancies, start-ups, technology providers and transport operators. You already know your own deliverables — a research brief, a figure in a client note, a tender response, a slide for the client — and you have probably used a chatbot for small things, without a method for using it on an engagement. No technical background is needed: the course requires no code, no API access and no IT support. You read and check what the assistant produces; you do not write the code it runs.

How the Course Is Organised

Modules 1 and 2 are the working basis: how the assistant behaves on real work, and what must not leave the browser. Modules 3 to 5 follow the engagement in the order the work is produced — research into an evidence file, figures from the exports, then the tender response those two feed. Module 6 is the set of files and checks that hold the engagement together.

  1. M1
    How Your Assistant Behaves on Real WorkText generation as prediction, not lookup; the knowledge cutoff; web search and research mode; what a long tender pack does to the context window; grounding an answer on a file you attached.
  2. M2
    Wrong Answers and What Leaves the BrowserFabrication and confident wrong answers; sycophantic agreement with your framing; reading a tool's data-use terms; classifying material as pasteable, redact-first, or never-paste.
  3. M3
    Building the Evidence FileA claim whose cited source does not support it; auditing citations; recording each claim with its source, a quote, and a date.
  4. M4
    Checkable Figures from Messy ExportsThe computation log; cleaning a messy export and confirming the change list; consolidating exports on a stated key; a chart in which every plotted figure traces to a cell; one check — read the log, reconcile one figure, stop at the first step that breaks.
  5. M5
    From Requirements to a Client SlideSchema-first extraction of the tender pack into a page-cited requirement matrix; a proposal section grounded in the matrix, the evidence file and the checked figure; scoring that draft against the published rubric; a slide outline with one message per slide and the figures carried unchanged.
  6. M6
    Holding an Engagement TogetherOne shared project per client, holding four files you own and keeping never-paste material out; a repeatable batch workflow with a coverage check; the decision log; the one client disclosure line that log supports.

What the Course Includes

Module quizzes

A short multiple-choice check at the end of each module, with feedback on every option so a wrong answer teaches something.

What You Need

  • A browser chat assistant on a free tier — the kind your organisation already allows. The course uses no paid features, plug-ins or code. The techniques are the same across mainstream assistants; a product is named only where it is the current way to do something the course teaches.
  • A spreadsheet, a PDF viewer, and the office software you already work with.
  • Your own engagement, when it is safe to use. The lessons run on one worked engagement. You will get the most out of them by repeating each task on material from a live one.
Before you paste anything

Do not enter client, partner, or unpublished material into a public assistant until you have completed Module 2. It shows you how to read a tool's data-use terms and how to sort a file into pasteable, redact-first, and never-paste. Until then, practise on published documents only.

How to Get the Most Out of It

  • Follow the modules in order. Modules 3 to 5 assume the checking habits of the modules before them, and they use the evidence file, the consolidated table and the matrix those modules build.
  • Keep the record. The evidence file and the decision log are what you show a client, a reviewer, or your own future self: what the tool produced, and what you checked.
  • Take the quizzes without notes. They are there to tell you which technique has not stuck yet.

What This Course Does Not Cover

The course is about the next action on an engagement, so it leaves out what does not change it: how language models are built or trained; writing or running code yourself; catalogues of prompting patterns; statistics beyond totals, averages, rates and comparisons; slide design and the craft of presenting; municipal document work and citizen-data rules; AI Act compliance, procuring AI systems, or comparing vendors; and the mobility method itself — fleet optimisation, service design, how a KPI is defined, or which measure to recommend. Those belong to your profession. This course is about getting a checkable figure or document out of the assistant you already have.

Ready to start?

Begin with Module 1. Its first lessons settle where an answer comes from — training memory, web search, or a file you attached — the boundary every later task is built on.
