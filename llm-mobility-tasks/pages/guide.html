Welcome & Course Guide

From Chatbot to Trusted Workflow: A Document-Production Course for Municipal Mobility Officers

Urban mobility Municipal document work Browser chat assistants

Welcome! In From Chatbot to Trusted Workflow, you'll learn how to do your real document tasks — summarising a SUMP, pulling figures out of a monitoring report, drafting a section of a funding proposal, working through a batch of citizen feedback — with the browser chat assistant you already have, and to do them in a way you can defend. Every lesson starts from a named task on a real mobility artefact and works it step by step: what we are doing, why, and what the assistant is doing with what we give it. By the end you'll know which parts of a task the assistant can carry, which parts stay yours to verify, and how to make sure nothing reaches an official document that you cannot trace to a source.

What You'll Achieve

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  • Predict where an assistant will go wrong on a given task before relying on its answer — and tell apart the failures its tools already cover (arithmetic via calculation, recent events via search, supplied documents via grounding) from those that remain.
  • Assemble a traceable evidence file on a mobility question with the assistant's research capabilities, recording for each source where it came from and why it can be trusted.
  • Extract the facts a task needs from a long official document into a structured table in which every value cites the page it came from.
  • Draft a document section grounded in supplied material, so that it states no figure, name or commitment the material does not contain.
  • Detect fabricated citations, invented numbers and overclaimed capabilities in assistant-drafted text before the text is used.
  • Improve a draft against an explicit rubric, scoring it and revising from the scores.
  • Recognise when a rubric-and-revise loop has started flattering the draft instead of improving it, and restart it from a clean context.
  • Rewrite a document for a different audience and reading level without changing the facts it states.
  • Decide what may and may not be entered into a public assistant when the material is unpublished, personal or confidential.

Who This Course Is For

Municipal urban-mobility staff who produce documents: officers and planners who write committee papers, monitoring reports, funding proposals and responses to citizens. You already know your own document conventions, at least one mobility document type, and the working vocabulary of EU funding. You have probably used a chatbot for small things — rewriting an email, summarising a page — without a method for using it systematically. No technical background is needed: the course requires no code, no API access and no IT support.

How the Course Is Organised

Six modules, 24 core lessons, plus module introductions, recaps and worked examples. Module 1 is the foundation — how the assistant actually works. Modules 2 to 6 follow the order in which a document gets produced: collect research, extract from official documents, draft grounded, verify and revise, adapt and deliver.

  1. M1
    How Your Assistant Actually WorksWhat a free-tier account has and what happens to text you paste in; text generation as prediction, not lookup; the calculation tool; hallucination as a predictable failure mode.
  2. M2
    Collecting Research: Building a Traceable Evidence FileWeb search for recent and local facts; a multi-source research pass with a deep-research tool; TRAAP-style source evaluation; verifying a citation before trusting it; recording provenance for each source.
  3. M3
    Extracting from Official Documents: From a SUMP to a Cited TableGrounding on supplied documents; context-window limits on long files; how an upload is read; citing PDF-viewer page numbers; writing a scoped prompt; extracting facts into a table with page citations.
  4. M4
    Drafting Grounded: Saying Only What the Source SaysSpot-checking extracted values against the original; instructing the assistant to draft only from supplied material.
  5. M5
    Verifying and Revising a DraftSycophancy — why the model agrees with and flatters you; scoring a draft against an explicit rubric; restarting from a clean context to break flattery drift.
  6. M6
    Adapting and Delivering: Plain Language and Personal DataRewriting for plain language without changing facts; checking that a rewrite kept every fact; recognising personal data before it goes in; checking a lawful basis before processing it.

What the Course Includes

Video lessons

Short, task-first lectures. Each explains an idea briefly, then solves a real mobility task with it, thinking out loud. Every module opens with an introduction and closes with a recap.

Worked examples

In Modules 2 to 6, step-by-step pages that carry out a full task on real, cited sources — the exact prompts used, the assistant's output, and the checks made on it.

Module quizzes

A short multiple-choice check at the end of each module, with feedback on every option so a wrong answer teaches something.

Final exam

Questions drawn across the modules, to confirm you can choose the right technique for a task you have not seen before.

What You Need

  • A browser chat assistant on a free tier — the kind your organisation already allows. The course uses no paid features, plug-ins or code.
  • A PDF viewer and the office software you already work with.
  • Your own material, when it is safe to use. The lessons work on real mobility artefacts; you will get the most out of them by repeating each task on a document from your own desk.
Before you paste anything

Do not enter unpublished, personal or confidential material into a public assistant until you have completed Module 1 — it shows you how to read a vendor's data-use statement — and Module 6, which covers personal data and lawful basis. Until then, practise on published documents only.

How to Get the Most Out of It

  • Follow the modules in order. Each task-family module assumes the assistant mechanics from Module 1 and the checking habits of the module before it.
  • Do the task alongside the lesson. Open your assistant in a second window and run the prompts yourself; the point is the workflow, not the video.
  • Read the worked examples slowly. They show the checks — clause by clause, value by value — that separate a usable draft from a fluent one.
  • Keep a record. For every fact you take from an assistant, note the source and page. That record is what you show your manager, an auditor or a funder.
  • Take the quizzes without notes. They are there to tell you which technique has not stuck yet.

What This Course Does Not Cover

The course is about the officer's next action, so it leaves out what does not change it: how language models are built or trained; building pipelines, agents or local tooling; procuring AI systems or comparing vendors; and the mobility method itself — how to calculate an indicator, run a cost-benefit analysis, design a measure or evaluate whether it worked. Those belong to your profession; this course is about producing the documents that carry them.

Ready to start?

Begin with Module 1. Its first lesson settles what an assistant can and cannot be trusted with — the boundary every later task is built on.
