MobiLLITY

Rewriting for plain language without changing facts — worked example

After working through this example you will be able to

  • Mark up a bureaucratic passage by hand for long sentences, formal phrasing, and passive constructions before rewriting it.
  • Write a rewriting prompt that names the task, supplies the source passage, and states a hard constraint forbidding any added, dropped, or altered fact, figure, name, or qualifier.
  • Require an assistant to flag anything it cannot simplify without risking a change in meaning, rather than let it guess.
  • Verify a plain-language rewrite sentence by sentence against its source, catching figures, names, or qualifiers that drifted before publication.

Following on from the bus-stop accessibility example, this time the passage comes from Gdańsk's own 2022 monitoring report for Programme VIII (Mobility and Transport). The manager wants a short plain-language item for the city's 'How is Gdańsk moving?' web page, drawn from the paragraph about 2022 traffic-calming and pedestrian work under Operational Goal VIII.1.

She has been burned before: a colleague let a chatbot 'clean up' a paragraph and it quietly dropped a figure, forcing a public retraction. So she wants two things — a rewritten paragraph she can publish, and a note she can staple to it if anyone asks 'did anything change?'

The task is to open the source report, locate the exact paragraph under 'Cel operacyjny VIII.1. Poprawa warunków dla ruchu pieszego i rowerowego', mark it up by hand the way the preceding lecture demonstrated, write the actual rewriting prompt with the hard fact-preservation constraint, produce the rewrite and a flagged-changes note, and then verify every sentence against the original — checking every number (17 km, 2.9 km, 66.4%, 12 km, 754, 18,000, 6.8 million, 13.8%), every named measure, and every qualifier such as 'rekordową' (record).

A key trap built into this task: tactile paving is not mentioned anywhere in this paragraph. It belongs to a different (accessibility) topic entirely, and letting it creep in from assumption rather than from the text would be exactly the kind of silent drift the verification step exists to catch.

Step 1 — Locate the exact source passage

The source passage was confirmed verbatim in the City of Gdańsk 2022 monitoring report, under Cel operacyjny VIII.1, running from the sentence about pedestrian-infrastructure work through the closing sentence about the 13.8% year-on-year rise in cycling counts.[1]

W 2022 r. prowadzono działania polegające na poprawie infrastruktury dla ruchu pieszego oraz na niwelowaniu negatywnego wpływu parkowania pojazdów na warunki poruszania się pieszo. Zbudowano lub zmodernizowano 17 km chodników, ciągów i stref pieszych. [...] W 2022 r. w tych punktach odnotowano rekordową liczbę przejazdów – ponad 6,8 mln, co oznacza wzrost o 13,8% w stosunku do poprzedniego roku.[1]

Because the two method sources for this exercise are English-language guides, the markup and rewrite were carried out on an English working translation of the passage, then every number was verified back against the original Polish. A Polish-language publication would repeat the same four-step process directly on the Polish text — the technique transfers even though the specific English word-swaps do not.

Step 2 — Hand-markup: long sentences, formal phrasing, passive voice

Splitting the translated passage into its 11 sentences and counting words identified three sentences over GOV.UK's 25-word guideline for splitting sentences: the one about slower traffic's benefits, the one about the 2.9 km addition to the traffic-calmed zone, and the one about cycling-traffic monitoring locations.[3]

Try to split up sentences that are over 25 words long.[3]

Two of those long sentences were also passive: 'A further 2.9 km of streets were included in the traffic-calmed zone...' and 'cycling-traffic monitoring, which is carried out at 30 locations...'. The closing sentence also carried a formal connector worth swapping: 'an increase of 13.8% in relation to the previous year'.

For the formal-phrase swap, the EC's KISS substitution table gives the pattern directly: connectors like 'concerning', 'regarding' and 'relating to' collapse to 'on' or 'about'.[2]

concerning, regarding, relating to → on[2]

Applying that logic, 'an increase of 13.8% in relation to the previous year' becomes 'than the previous year' — same comparison, no dangling formal connector.

For the passive-to-active conversion, GOV.UK's guidance gives the exact pattern to follow: name the doer and put it first.[3]

it's easier to read 'You need an electronic travel authorisation' than 'An electronic travel authorisation is needed'[3]

Since the whole report is the City of Gdańsk describing its own programme actions, the doer for 'A further 2.9 km of streets were included in the traffic-calmed zone' is the city itself: 'Gdańsk added a further 2.9 km of streets to the traffic-calmed zone.' The EC guide makes the same underlying point about naming the agent.[2]

If you change passive verb forms into active ones, your writing will become clearer because you will be forced to name the agent — the person, organisation or thing that is carrying out the action.[2]

One passive construction was deliberately left alone: the monitoring sentence ('We know this thanks to cycling-traffic monitoring, which is carried out at 30 locations...') names no agent in the source. Inventing 'the city monitors...' there would add a fact not in the passage. GOV.UK itself allows passive precisely for this situation.[3]

when the outcome is more important than the agent of the action – for example, 'Biometric residence permits have been replaced by eVisas'[3]

Step 3 — Write the rewriting prompt

Task: Rewrite the passage below in plain language for residents, for publication on Gdańsk's 'How is Gdańsk moving?' web page. Target reading level: a busy resident with no transport-planning background should understand it in one read-through.

Material (rewrite this passage only — do not draw on any other section of the report):
"In 2022, work was carried out to improve pedestrian infrastructure and to reduce the negative impact of vehicle parking on conditions for walking. 17 km of pavements, walkways and pedestrian zones were built or upgraded. Prioritising pedestrian traffic in district centres was also important, increasing comfort and safety for pedestrians and cyclists by calming car traffic. Slower traffic on most of the city's streets means fewer accidents, less noise and a greater sense of safety, alongside smoother traffic flow and similar journey times. A further 2.9 km of streets were included in the traffic-calmed zone, which by the end of the year already covered 66.4% of public roads in Gdańsk. Recent years have been a period of intensive expansion and modernisation of cycling infrastructure. In 2022, more than 12 km of cycle routes in total were built or upgraded. The number of bicycle parking spaces increased by 754, to 18,000. Improvements to cycling infrastructure translate into the growing popularity of bikes. We know this thanks to cycling-traffic monitoring, which is carried out at 30 locations in Gdańsk, 12 of which are reference points for measuring cycling traffic. In 2022, these points recorded a record number of journeys, more than 6.8 million, which represents an increase of 13.8% in relation to the previous year."

Hard constraints (non-negotiable):
1. Do not add, drop, or change any fact, figure, name, date, unit, or qualifying word (e.g. "record," "further," "more than") from the passage above.
2. Keep every number exactly as given: 17 km, 2.9 km, 66.4%, 12 km, 754, 18,000, 6.8 million, 13.8%.
3. Do not introduce any measure, project, or place name that is not already in the passage (for example, do not mention tactile paving, kerb-lowering, or any other accessibility measure — none of that is in this passage).
4. Prefer short sentences (under 25 words) and active voice, naming the city as the agent only where the passage's own context makes that unambiguous.
5. If any sentence cannot be simplified without risking a change in meaning, do not guess — leave a note flagging exactly which phrase and why.

Output format: (a) the rewritten passage; (b) a short "flagged changes" note listing anything you simplified with residual uncertainty, anything you could not simplify, and any word-choice judgement calls you made.

Step 4 — The rewrite and its flagged-changes note

In 2022, Gdańsk worked to make walking easier and safer, including tackling cars parked on pavements. The city built or upgraded 17 km of pavements, walkways and pedestrian zones. It also gave pedestrians more priority in district centres by slowing down car traffic. Slower traffic on most city streets means fewer accidents, less noise and a greater sense of safety. It also means smoother traffic flow, with journey times staying about the same. The city added a further 2.9 km of streets to its traffic-calmed zone. By the end of the year, that zone covered 66.4% of Gdańsk's public roads. Cycling infrastructure has also been expanding fast in recent years. In 2022, Gdańsk built or upgraded more than 12 km of cycle routes. The number of bike parking spaces rose by 754, to 18,000. Better cycling infrastructure is helping more people choose bikes. We know this because cycling traffic is monitored at 30 locations in Gdańsk, 12 of which are reference measuring points. In 2022, these points recorded a record 6.8 million-plus bike trips, 13.8% more than the year before.

Flagged-changes note: an agent ('Gdańsk'/'the city') was added to sentences describing building work, since the whole report is the city describing its own actions — no new fact, just an implicit actor made explicit. The monitoring sentence deliberately keeps its passive form because the source names no agent for who runs the monitoring. 'An increase of 13.8% in relation to the previous year' was shortened to '13.8% more than the year before' — same percentage, same reference year. 'Reference measuring points' was flagged as technical monitoring jargon that could not be simplified further without either lengthening the sentence or guessing at what makes those 12 points 'reference' points.

Step 5 — Sentence-by-sentence verification against the original

Each rewritten sentence was placed next to its source clause and every number, name, and qualifier checked off: 17 km, 2.9 km, 66.4%, 12 km, 754, 18,000, 6.8 million, and 13.8% were all confirmed present and unchanged, as was the qualifier 'record' (rekordową) attached to the 6.8 million count.[1]

Two drifts surfaced during this check. First, the source names both pedestrians and cyclists as beneficiaries of the district-centre traffic-calming — 'podniesienie komfortu oraz bezpieczeństwa pieszych i rowerzystów' — but the draft rewrite mentioned only pedestrians, silently dropping cyclists.[1]

podniesienie komfortu oraz bezpieczeństwa pieszych i rowerzystów przez uspokajanie ruchu samochodów[1]

Second, the source describes cycling infrastructure work as 'intensywnej rozbudowy i modernizacji' — expansion and modernisation, i.e. both new routes and upgrades to existing ones — but the draft compressed this to 'expanding fast', losing the explicit modernisation half.[1]

okres intensywnej rozbudowy i modernizacji infrastruktury rowerowej[1]

Both were fixed before publication: 'more comfort and safety' was restored to cover both pedestrians and cyclists, and 'expanding fast' was corrected to 'expanding and being upgraded fast' to keep the two distinct actions.

Step 6 — Corrected final rewrite, ready to publish

In 2022, Gdańsk worked to make walking easier and safer, including tackling cars parked on pavements. The city built or upgraded 17 km of pavements, walkways and pedestrian zones. It also gave both pedestrians and cyclists more comfort and safety in district centres by slowing down car traffic. Slower traffic on most city streets means fewer accidents, less noise and a greater sense of safety. It also means smoother traffic flow, with journey times staying about the same. The city added a further 2.9 km of streets to its traffic-calmed zone. By the end of the year, that zone covered 66.4% of Gdańsk's public roads. Cycling infrastructure has also been expanding and being upgraded fast in recent years. In 2022, Gdańsk built or upgraded more than 12 km of cycle routes. The number of bike parking spaces rose by 754, to 18,000. Better cycling infrastructure is helping more people choose bikes. We know this because cycling traffic is monitored at 30 locations in Gdańsk, 12 of which are reference measuring points. In 2022, these points recorded a record 6.8 million-plus bike trips, 13.8% more than the year before.

A final scripted check confirmed all eight figures, the qualifier 'record', and the absence of any accessibility measure such as tactile paving in the corrected rewrite — confirming that this specific paragraph never contained that term to begin with, but that the check is what proves it rather than assumption.

Takeaways

  • Hand-marking the source first — circling long sentences, naming a formal-phrase swap, marking a passive-to-active conversion — gives the rewriting prompt concrete targets instead of a vague 'make this simpler' instruction.
  • The rewriting prompt must name the task, paste the exact source passage, and state the hard constraint explicitly: no fact, figure, name, or qualifier may be added, dropped, or changed, and anything unsimplifiable without risk must be flagged rather than guessed.
  • A fluent rewrite can silently drop a named beneficiary (cyclists) or thin a two-part claim (expansion and modernisation) down to one part, even while every number stays correct — only a sentence-by-sentence check against the original catches that kind of drift.
  • Not every passive construction should be converted: where the source itself names no agent, forcing one in risks adding a fact that isn't there, so active voice is used only where the context makes the doer unambiguous.
  • Verifying against the original also confirms what is absent — checking that no assumed detail (such as tactile paving from an unrelated accessibility paragraph) crept into a rewrite it was never part of.
